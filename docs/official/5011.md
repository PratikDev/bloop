## How

- Assets (`docs/official/5011_assets.md`): the rule's movie is the 1920×1080 sphere view, which has no flat map; the page's Science On a Sphere movie (iss_lightning_sos_yearly_07312023_2048p60.mp4) is a 2:1 map of the same events and is the one decoded (override recorded). Every one of its 6002 frames was streamed through ffmpeg into memory; nothing was written to disk.
- Georeference: the drawn equator and tropic lines sit at 23.47, 0.09, -23.47° (expected 23.44, 0, −23.44); the frame's edges peak against Natural Earth coastline edges at shift (0, 0) px; a texture land mask scores IoU 0.659 (the flat-water base map defeats a brightness mask, so the IoU is reported, not used).
- Marks: a pixel is lightning when min(R, G) − B > 60. A flash is counted once, when its mark appears (lit now, not lit within 2 px in the previous frame); a fused mark larger than 200 px counts as area ÷ 6 (the median single mark). 450,054 marks; 0.1% were fused clusters.
- Frame times: the movie has no date and does not run at a constant rate (between anchors 0.23–0.61 days per frame). The marks' mean latitude per frame swings with the seasons; its maxima and minima are dated to the days on which the independent LIS/OTD monthly climatology peaks and bottoms out, the first and last active frames to the period's ends (14 anchors), and time is linear between them. Approximate.
- Window: an isolated mark stays lit for 55 frames (median), about 30 days at the model's rate, against the SVS text's 10-day window.
- Source version: the SVS page lists 'Quality Controlled LIS on ISS Science Data V1'; the checks use V3 (DOI 10.5067/LIS/ISSLIS/DATA114, isslis_v3_fin), the current reprocessing, over 1 Mar 2017 (its first day) to 31 Jul 2023 (the movie's end); V2 and V3 both end 16 Nov 2023.
- View time (added 30 Oct): ISS LIS is also read as a flash rate, flashes per hour of effective view time in each box, which removes the station's uneven revisit. Per-region Spearman with rates: Bangladesh 0.59, Congo basin 0.40, Lake Maracaibo -0.23, Himalayan foothills 0.36 (counts: Bangladesh 0.59, Congo basin 0.34, Lake Maracaibo -0.20, Himalayan foothills 0.36).
- The 60–90°E gap of the near-real-time plots is not in the quality-controlled data: view time there is 1.00× the mean of the neighbouring 30° bands (median over the 12 windows), so it does not explain the weak Bangladesh agreement.

## Known limits

- Dates are approximate (seasonal anchors), so counts per 10-day window carry timing error; the region check uses windows chosen by date before any frame was dated.
- Dense storms fuse marks; fused clusters are counted by area, which undercounts where marks overlap completely.
- ISS LIS sees ±52° of latitude only on orbit passes; the frames show what LIS saw, so outside its coverage there is nothing to hear.
- Stored at 1° and 1 window(s) per step to stay under the 25 MB budget; the Bangladesh box keeps every mark (1,287 marks).
