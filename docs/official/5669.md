## What it is (SVS text)

Aerosols are tiny solid or liquid particles that float in the atmosphere and can travel long distances, affecting air quality and visibility far from their sources. This visualization still, captured for August 19, 2025 (20:00 UTC), is based on NASA's Goddard Earth Observing System (GEOS) model. The GEOS model delivers realistic, high-resolution weather and aerosol data that enable customized environmental prediction and advances in AI research.

## How

- Assets (`docs/official/5669_assets.md`): the largest still ≤ 7680 px wide (7680×4320) and its key; 5572 / 5552 are the animated 2024 versions of the same rendering and are not used for values.
- Key: four swatches read from the key image (smoke (210, 94, 69); dust (193, 154, 220); sea salt (133, 170, 169); sulfate (83, 220, 52)); each is named by matching its hue to the colour words of the SVS text.
- Georeference: the still is the whole globe stretched to 16:9. 91.6% of its bare grey base-map pixels fall on Natural Earth land at the full-globe bounds (lower at ±1° and ±2°).
- Type: CIELAB hue nearest a key colour, for pixels whose chroma is above the open ocean's 99th percentile (18.9); pale pixels brighter than the ocean's 99th-percentile lightness (37.6) in the sea-salt hue sector are sea salt. Density: lightness above the ocean's median, 0–1, relative. Shares of all pixels: smoke 1.1%, dust 2.6%, sea salt 1.5%, sulfate 20.0%, none 74.7%.

## Known limits

- The key has no numbers: density is relative, and the record never states an optical depth.
- Colours mix where types overlap and where a faint tint sits on the dark ocean; the hue rule assigns each pixel one type. The hand-label confusion check measures that error once 200 labels per class exist (0 so far).
- The comparison is with MERRA-2, a different model in the GEOS family: GEOS-FP's own archive for the day has rolled off.
- Smoke and sulfate both play on the tone synth an octave apart (the app has three synth kinds for four types).
