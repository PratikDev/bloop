## What it is (SVS text)

Located 40,000 light-years from Earth in the constellation Columba, this globular cluster is called Caldwell 73 or NGC 1851. A globular cluster is a spherical group of stars that are gravitationally bound to each other, with most of the stars concentrated at the cluster’s center.  As the radar scans around in this sonification, the radius of the stars is mapped to pitch, so stars farther from the center are higher pitched. The entire image is converted to the sound of a choir, while the orange and red stars are represented by a marimba, and the blue stars are represented by a glockenspiel.Credit: SYSTEM Sounds (M. Russo, A. Santaguida)For more information about the Hubble Space Telescope and its images, visit https://nasa.gov/hubble. ||

## How

- Asset: the 1920×1920 movie (1020 frames at 30 fps, 34 s, AAC stereo), streamed through ffmpeg; the soundtrack is decoded to mono 22.05 kHz and deleted after analysis. No media is shipped.
- Sweep: the only moving thing is the white line, found as each frame minus the movie's median frame; the centre is the intersection of the fitted lines (959, 959 px) and the line turns at -12.0 °/s.
- Stars: local maxima of the median frame, classed by hue: red/orange 907, blue 53, white 25,204.
- Three measurements of the SVS text's 'radius → pitch' mapping: (a) pYIN f0 vs the one coloured star the sweep crosses (planned; Spearman -0.14, n 219); (b) the pitch of each note onset (constant-Q, new energy) vs the brightest coloured star crossed within ±2 frames (Spearman 0.04, n 110); (c) the same onsets vs the star the movie circles as it plays (Spearman -0.08, n 90).
- Timbre: spectral centroid at single-star crossings: red/orange stars 762 Hz, blue stars 858 Hz (SVS: marimba and glockenspiel).
- Engine: a generic radial-sweep traversal (src/lib/audio-adapter/radial-sweep.ts, unit tests): centre, angular speed and radius → pitch (exponential or linear) or radius → time (a growing ring).

## Known limits

- The stated mapping is not recovered: none of the three measurements shows a relation between radius and pitch. The choir voices the whole line at once, so pitch trackers follow it, and the field is so dense that pairing a note with one star is ambiguous. The fitted lines are stored (mapping_fit.json) but none is supported.
- This item is method evidence, not Earth data; it has no map layer and is not in the Official tab's list.
